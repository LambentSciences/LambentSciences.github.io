LAMBENT SCIENCES
CONCEPT PROPOSAL 02

TITLE
Compact Mid-Infrared Laser Source for Portable Gas Sensing

PREPARED BY
Lambent Sciences (CEO: Bhabana Pati)

STATUS
Concept-stage proposal. Figures and durations below are planning estimates to be confirmed during feasibility.

1. SUMMARY
Many gases of interest have strong absorption features in the mid-infrared. We propose a compact, low-power mid-infrared laser source packaged for portable, battery-friendly gas detection, from concept through a field-testable prototype.

2. PROBLEM
Laboratory-grade mid-infrared sensing is accurate but bulky, power-hungry, and costly. Portable alternatives often trade away selectivity or sensitivity. There is room for a small source that keeps the benefits of direct absorption spectroscopy.

3. PROPOSED APPROACH
- Select a compact mid-infrared laser type suited to the target gas absorption line.
- Package the source with thermal control and drive electronics sized for portable use.
- Pair it with a short-path or multipass sample cell and a detector.
- Use wavelength modulation or a similar technique to improve sensitivity and reject background.
- Calibrate against reference gas mixtures.

4. EXPECTED BENEFITS
- Selective detection based on a gas's own absorption signature.
- Small size and low power for handheld or fixed-site monitoring.
- A platform that can be retargeted to other gases by changing the source wavelength.

5. PROGRAM PLAN (CONCEPT TO PROTOTYPE)
Phase 1 - Concept (about 1 month): Choose target gas, concentration range, and environment.
Phase 2 - Feasibility (about 2-3 months): Evaluate source options, estimate sensitivity, and define the sample cell approach.
Phase 3 - Design (about 3 months): Design optics, thermal management, electronics, and enclosure.
Phase 4 - Build (about 3 months): Assemble the prototype sensor.
Phase 5 - Test (about 2-3 months): Characterize sensitivity, drift, and cross-sensitivity using reference gases.
Phase 6 - Handoff (about 1 month): Deliver test data, documentation, and a roadmap to a field pilot.

6. DELIVERABLES
- Working prototype gas sensor with its laser source.
- Characterization report covering sensitivity, response time, and stability.
- Design documentation and recommended next steps.

7. KEY RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
- Source availability or performance at the needed wavelength: survey suppliers during feasibility and keep a fallback option.
- Temperature drift affecting calibration: include active thermal control and drift testing across the expected operating range.
- Interference from other gases or humidity: select absorption lines carefully and test against likely interferents.
- Laser safety: classify and label the device appropriately and review with a qualified laser safety officer.

8. NEXT STEP
Confirm the target gas and operating environment, then begin Phase 1.
