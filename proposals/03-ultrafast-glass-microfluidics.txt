LAMBENT SCIENCES
CONCEPT PROPOSAL 03

TITLE
Ultrafast Laser Micromachining of Glass Microfluidic Devices

PREPARED BY
Lambent Sciences (CEO: Bhabana Pati)

STATUS
Concept-stage proposal. Figures and durations below are planning estimates to be confirmed during feasibility.

1. SUMMARY
Ultrafast lasers can modify transparent materials such as glass with very little heat spread, which makes them well suited to forming microchannels and features directly inside a glass substrate. We propose a direct-write fabrication workflow for microfluidic devices, developed from concept to a demonstrable prototype chip.

2. PROBLEM
Conventional microfluidic fabrication relies on masks and multi-step processing, which makes early design iteration slow and expensive. Teams want to test a new channel layout in days, not weeks.

3. PROPOSED APPROACH
- Use an ultrafast laser with a focusing and motion system to write channel patterns directly from a digital design file.
- Combine laser modification with a follow-on step (such as selective etching) where needed to open the channels.
- Develop process recipes linking laser parameters to channel size, smoothness, and repeatability.
- Demonstrate sealed, leak-tested devices with fluid inlets and outlets.

4. EXPECTED BENEFITS
- No photomasks, so layout changes go from file to part quickly.
- Three-dimensional channel layouts that are hard to achieve with planar methods.
- Glass offers good chemical resistance and optical transparency for sensing and imaging.

5. PROGRAM PLAN (CONCEPT TO PROTOTYPE)
Phase 1 - Concept (about 1 month): Define the target application and the channel geometry requirements.
Phase 2 - Feasibility (about 2 months): Run initial parameter studies on sample glass and assess channel quality.
Phase 3 - Design (about 2 months): Design the test device, fluid interfaces, and inspection plan.
Phase 4 - Build (about 2-3 months): Fabricate prototype chips and refine the process recipe.
Phase 5 - Test (about 2 months): Measure dimensional accuracy, surface quality, flow performance, and leak tightness.
Phase 6 - Handoff (about 1 month): Deliver process documentation, test data, and a plan for scaling production.

6. DELIVERABLES
- Prototype glass microfluidic devices.
- Documented laser and post-processing recipe.
- Test report and recommendations for pilot-scale fabrication.

7. KEY RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
- Channel wall roughness affects flow or optics: tune pulse and scan parameters and evaluate post-processing options early.
- Throughput too low for the target use: define throughput goals in feasibility and consider parallel or hybrid processing.
- Substrate variability between glass batches: qualify glass sources and record batch information with each device.
- Laser safety: design the workstation with appropriate enclosure and interlocks and review with a qualified laser safety officer.

8. NEXT STEP
Select a representative microfluidic design, then begin Phase 1.
